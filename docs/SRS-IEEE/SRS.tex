\section{Introduction} 
% ==============================================================

This document is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for \progname{}, a mobile game
that prepares children aged 4 to 7 years for paediatric ophthalmology appointments.
This document defines the expected behaviour, scope, and requirements of \progname{},
establishing a basis for implementation, verification, and validation by describing what the system
is expected to accomplish and the conditions under which it will be used.

This document follows the IEEE SRS template provided in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate},
with guidance from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 \cite{ISOIECIEEE291482018} for requirements engineering and
ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 \cite{ISOIECIEEE152882023} for systems life-cycle management. The team made
modifications to the original template to better suit the project's needs. In Section~\ref{non-func-req}
(Non-Functional Requirements), the requirements are organized according to the Volere template provided
in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate}. The AI and Machine Learning Requirements section was
also removed, as it is not applicable to the project.

\subsection{Purpose}

The purpose of \progname{} is to reduce anxiety and uncertainty in children before paediatric
ophthalmology appointments by providing accessible, age-appropriate preparation for common eye
examination procedures. \progname{} aims to improve children's preparedness and willingness to participate while helping
parents and healthcare professionals prepare them for appointments. Intended outcomes include
reduced anxiety, improved cooperation, sustained engagement across the target age range,
more effective preparation at home, and greater clinical efficiency. The application complements,
rather than replaces, professional guidance and medical examinations.

\subsection{Scope} \label{sec_Scope}

\progname{} is a mobile application intended to help children aged 4 to 7 years become familiar
with common paediatric ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment. The application
will present preparation activities as age-appropriate interactive games that allow children to
practice actions and learn what they may experience during an eye examination.

The primary users are children preparing for ophthalmology appointments, with parents or guardians
supporting their use of the application. Ophthalmologists and child-life specialists are secondary
stakeholders who may provide clinical and developmental guidance during development and may use
the application as part of a broader preparation process.

The application will provide interactive activities corresponding to common ophthalmology
procedures, provide feedback during activities, track completion of preparation activities, and
allow users to repeat activities when needed. The application will be designed to support
preparation before an appointment, including preparation in a home environment.

The expected benefits are increased familiarity with ophthalmology procedures, reduced uncertainty
and anxiety, improved cooperation during examinations, sustained engagement, and potentially
shorter or less difficult clinical appointments.

\progname{} will not diagnose medical conditions, provide medical advice, replace an ophthalmologist,
or determine whether a child requires a particular medical procedure. The application will not
replace an actual clinical examination. Medical decisions will remain the responsibility of qualified
healthcare professionals.

The initial system will focus on preparation for common ophthalmology procedures and will not
attempt to cover every possible procedure or clinical situation. Features identified as stretch
goals, such as augmented reality simulations and personalized preparation based on appointment
information, are outside the minimum required scope of the initial system.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Paediatric ophthalmology appointments can be stressful and unfamiliar for children.
Procedures such as vision testing, eye drops, and slit-lamp examinations may cause anxiety, particularly when children do not know what to expect.
This anxiety can make it difficult to cooperate during examinations, potentially affecting the quality and completeness of clinical assessments.
For example, a study of 298 children undergoing cycloplegic eye examinations found that 26\% experienced significant distress before receiving eye drops,
while uncooperative children were also more anxious and required more time to complete the examination \cite{ChiaEtAl2015}.

Children and their families therefore need adequate preparation for common ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment.
Without appropriate preparation, unfamiliar clinical experiences may contribute to increased anxiety, reduced cooperation, and longer or more difficult appointments.
This project addresses the need for an accessible, child-friendly way to help children become more familiar and comfortable with the paediatric ophthalmology experience.

\subsection{Product Vision}

\progname{} is an interactive mobile application designed to help
children aged 3--7 feel more comfortable and prepared for paediatric
ophthalmology appointments. Through age appropriate games and activities,
the application introduces children to common procedures, including
vision testing, eye drop administration, and slit lamp examinations.
By allowing children and their families to become familiar with these
procedures before their appointments, \progname{} aims to reduce
anxiety, improve cooperation during examinations, and support a
more efficient clinical experience.

The primary goals of \progname{} are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduce Children's Anxiety:}
    Help children understand what to expect during common ophthalmology
    procedures, reducing anxiety associated with unfamiliar clinical
    experiences.

    \item \textbf{Improve Children's Cooperation During Examinations:}
    Prepare children to participate more cooperatively in clinical
    examinations, reducing interruptions and repeated attempts
    during procedures.

    \item \textbf{Maintain Engagement Across Age Groups:}
    Provide engaging and age appropriate activities that encourage
    children across the target age range to actively participate
    in their preparation.

    \item \textbf{Enable Effective At-Home Preparation:}
    Allow children and their families to independently prepare
    for upcoming ophthalmology appointments through accessible
    and understandable activities.

    \item \textbf{Improve Clinical Efficiency:}
    Support more efficient ophthalmology examinations by improving
    children's preparedness and reducing difficulties associated
    with completing clinical procedures.
\end{itemize}

These goals establish the intended outcomes of \progname{} and provide
the foundation for the functional and non-functional requirements
defined in this document.

\subsection{System Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \progname{} shall operate on consumer smartphones and tablets running
    iOS or Android.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_MobilePlatforms})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Patient families and clinics may use mobile devices running
        either operating system. Supporting both enables preparation
        at home and in clinic without assuming a particular platform.
    \end{itemize}

    \item Third-party assets and libraries included in \progname{} shall have
    licenses allowing their use and redistribution within the application.
    Required copyright notices, licenses, and attributions shall be retained.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_ApplicationLicensing})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} The application's source code is licensed under the
        MIT license, while third-party materials have their own licensing.
        These conditions must be satisfied when the application is released.
    \end{itemize}

    \item Third-party materials present in the \progname{} GitHub
    repository shall have licenses allowing redistribution in the form committed to the repository.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_RepositoryLicensing})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Permission to distribute an asset in
        an application does not necessarily mean redistribution of its raw
        files in the project repository is permitted. Repository contents must satisfy
        the applicable licensing conditions (separately from the compiled application).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall be built using a game engine with native support
    for cross-platform deployment to iOS and Android.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_CrossPlatformEngine})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Native deployment support satisfies \conref{C_MobilePlatforms}. The team has
        currently selected the Unity game engine, but the selected engine may
        change provided that it satisfies this constraint.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall provide all included preparation modules and their
    required assets without internet connection after installation.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_OfflineOperation})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Families must be able to prepare at home and
        in clinic, where connectivity may be poor or unavailable.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall operate as a standalone local application and shall
    not initiate network communications during operation, including
    through included third-party libraries.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_NoNetworkCommunications})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require external
        services or data transit. Prohibiting network
        communications also prevents included libraries from transmitting usage
        data. Downloading and updating the application through its distribution
        channels are outside normal application operation (i.e., a patient progressing through the application's
        preparation modules).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall not request, collect, or store identifying
    information or clinical records, and shall not integrate with clinic
    information systems.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_PatientPrivacy})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require patient
        records. Limiting application data to local gameplay, like character selection and scores,
        supports preparation without requiring identifying information.
    \end{itemize}

    \item The iOS and Android releases of \progname{} shall comply with the Apple App Store and Google Play submission
    requirements, respectively, including applicable policies for applications meant for children, at time of submission.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_StoreCompliance})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} These marketplaces are the planned distribution
        channels through which families and clinics will install the application. Compliance
        with their submission requirements is necessary for distribution to the target users.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Use Cases}

The following use cases describe how children and caregivers use
\progname{} to explore ophthalmology preparation games through a
journey of levels. All procedure game levels remain accessible regardless
of whether earlier levels have been completed. Children may choose a
character, earn journey points, receive in game coins or cosmetic items,
and use owned items to customize their character. The personal scoreboard
shows the child's own progress, not a ranking against other children.
The reward amounts, character designs, available accessories, and specific
gameplay interactions described below are initial design proposals that
will be refined with the project team and clinical supervisors.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_FirstUse}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_FirstUse}: Complete Initial Application Setup}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Caregiver

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The application is installed on a supported device.
    \item The application is being opened for the first time, and no
    caregiver acknowledgment has been recorded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The caregiver opens \progname{}.
    \item The system displays a welcome screen explaining that the
    application helps children become familiar with eye examinations
    through interactive games.
    \item The system displays a notice stating that the application is
    an educational preparation aid and not a substitute for professional
    medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
    \item The caregiver reads the notice and selects the acknowledgment
    control.
    \item The system records the acknowledgment locally.
    \item The system displays an introduction to the journey, explaining
    that all procedure game levels are available and that children can
    earn points and cosmetic rewards as they play.
    \item The caregiver continues to the home screen, where the child
    can choose a character and start exploring the journey.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Notice Not Acknowledged:} If the caregiver does not
    acknowledge the notice, the system does not complete initial setup.
    \item \textbf{Setup Interrupted:} If the application closes before
    acknowledgment is saved, the system displays the notice again on
    the next launch.
    \item \textbf{Returning User:} If acknowledgment was previously
    recorded, the system opens the home screen without repeating setup.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The caregiver has been informed of the application's purpose
    and limitations.
    \item Acknowledgment is recorded locally, and the child can access
    the journey without creating an account.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_ChooseCharacter}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_ChooseCharacter}: Choose a Journey Character}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Initial application setup has been completed.
    \item The application is displaying the home screen or character
    selection screen.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child selects the character button on the home screen.
    \item The system displays the available starting characters with
    large visual previews.
    \item The child selects a character from the gallery.
    \item The system displays an enlarged preview of the selected
    character and a confirmation control.
    \item The child confirms the selection.
    \item The system saves the selected character identifier locally.
    \item The system returns to the home screen and displays the
    selected character beside the journey entrance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Try Another Character:} Before confirming, the child
    selects a different character; the system updates the preview.
    \item \textbf{Cancel Selection:} If the child returns home without
    confirming, the previously selected character remains active.
    \item \textbf{Change Later:} If a character was previously saved,
    the child can return to character selection and replace it without
    losing levels, points, coins, or owned accessories.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The selected character appears in the child's journey.
    \item Game progress and reward balances remain unchanged.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_SelectGame}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_SelectGame}: Select a Level on the Journey Map}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The application is open and initial setup is complete.
    \item The journey contains the Vision Testing, Eye-Drop Preparation,
    and Slit-Lamp Preparation levels.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child selects the journey button on the home screen.
    \item The system displays a journey map containing the procedure
    levels in a suggested order, with a label and illustration for each.
    \item The system shows each level's current status: Not Started,
    In Progress, or Completed.
    \item The child selects the Slit-Lamp Preparation level, even
    though the Vision Testing and Eye-Drop levels are unfinished.
    \item The system displays the slit-lamp level's title, an
    introductory illustration, and a play control.
    \item The child selects play.
    \item The system starts the slit-lamp level and presents its
    child-friendly instructions.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Select Another Level:} The child selects Vision Testing
    or Eye-Drop Preparation; the system opens that level's introduction.
    \item \textbf{Replay Completed Level:} The child selects a completed
    level; the system permits replay without resetting the completed
    status or reissuing the first completion reward.
    \item \textbf{Return Home:} The child selects home before pressing
    play; no level status or reward balance changes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The selected preparation level is open.
    \item No earlier level had to be completed or unlocked first.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_ProcedureInformation}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_ProcedureInformation}: View a Caregiver Procedure Guide}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Caregiver

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The application is open.
    \item The built-in caregiver guide contains explanations of the
    supported ophthalmology procedures.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The caregiver selects the caregiver guide on the home screen.
    \item The system lists Vision Testing, Eye-Drop Administration,
    and Slit-Lamp Examination.
    \item The caregiver selects Eye-Drop Administration.
    \item The system displays a clinician-reviewed explanation of why
    eye drops may be used and what a child may experience.
    \item The system explains that the Eye-Drop Preparation level uses
    a virtual character to show a clinician administering drops.
    \item The system displays the educational use disclaimer in the guide.
    \item The caregiver returns to the guide menu or home screen.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Different Procedure:} The caregiver selects Vision
    Testing or Slit-Lamp Examination; the matching guide is displayed.
    \item \textbf{Exit Guide:} Returning home does not affect the
    child's game progress, score, or rewards.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The caregiver has reviewed preparation information for
    the selected procedure.
    \item No diagnostic or treatment recommendation is produced.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\ucref{UC_VisionGame}: Play the Vision Testing Level}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}

    a vision test.
    \item The system explains the matching activity using narration
    and visual examples rather than requiring reading.
    \item The system displays a reference symbol and several
    possible matching symbols.
    \item The child selects the matching symbol or selects skip.
    \item The system gives encouraging feedback on the selection
    or acknowledges the skipped round without penalty.
    \item Steps 3--5 repeat with different symbols until every
    required matching round has been attempted or skipped.
    \item The system displays the end of level screen and records
    the Vision Testing level as Completed.
    \item The system initiates the first completion reward process
    described in \ucref{UC_CompleteGame}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incorrect Match:} The system offers another attempt
    or lets the child skip to the next symbol.
    \item \textbf{Skip Round:} The system advances to the next symbol
    without requiring a correct response.
    \item \textbf{Exit Early:} The child selects home before the final
    round, the system returns to the journey map and retains an
    In Progress status rather than marking the level Completed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If the end of level screen was reached, the level is
    marked Completed even when some rounds were skipped.
    \item The game does not measure visual acuity or provide
    clinical results.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Requirements}

This section describes the information managed by \progname{}
to support journey-based progression, character selection,
game completion tracking, and a personal scoreboard.
The application does not require patient accounts or
integration with clinical information systems. All user
data is maintained locally on the user's device.

Each ophthalmology procedure is represented by one
preparation game, which acts as a level in the child's
journey. Games may contain multiple smaller interactions
or rounds, but these are treated as part of the game
rather than as separate data entities.

All levels are accessible regardless of whether previous
levels have been completed. As children explore the games,
they can earn points, track their progress, and view their
achievements through a personal scoreboard.


\subsection{Major Data Entities}

The system manages the following major data entities:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Procedure Game:}
    Represents a game designed to familiarize children
    with a particular ophthalmology procedure, such as
    vision testing, eye-drop administration, or slit-lamp
    examinations. Each procedure has one corresponding
    game that represents a level in the child's journey.
    A game may contain multiple interactive exercises
    or rounds, and its content may vary according to
    the child's age group.

    \item \textbf{Character:}
    Represents a character available for the child to
    select and use throughout their preparation journey.
    Each character has a unique identifier, a display
    name, and a visual representation.

    \item \textbf{Local Preferences:}
    Represents locally stored application settings,
    including the child's optional nickname, selected
    character, age group, and display or accessibility
    preferences. These settings do not require a
    patient account.

    \item \textbf{Game Progress:}
    Represents the completion status and points earned
    for each preparation game. Progress is tracked at
    the game level rather than for individual rounds
    or exercises. This information reflects participation
    in the application rather than clinical performance
    or assessment results.

    \item \textbf{Journey Progress:}
    Represents the child's overall progression across
    all preparation games, including accumulated points
    and completed levels. This information is calculated
    from individual Game Progress records and displayed
    through the personal scoreboard.

\end{itemize}

The relationship between these entities is as follows:
each Procedure Game represents one ophthalmology procedure
and corresponds to one level in the journey. Local
Preferences identifies the character selected by the child
and determines how the application is presented.

Game Progress records the completion status and points
earned for each Procedure Game independently. Journey
Progress summarizes these records to display the child's
overall achievements without requiring duplicate
progress data to be stored.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}

\progname{} follows a data-minimization approach,
collecting and retaining only the information necessary
to support character selection, journey progression,
user preferences, and local game progress.
The application's privacy and data management
practices are outlined below:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Data Collection:}
    The application does not require patient accounts,
    medical records, appointment details, or personally
    identifying information. Optional nicknames are
    used solely for personalization and do not need
    to correspond to the child's legal name.

    \item \textbf{Character Selection:}
    Children may select a character to accompany
    them throughout their preparation journey.
    The application stores the identifier of the
    selected character locally without requiring
    personal information or account registration.

    \item \textbf{Game and Journey Progress:}
    The application stores the completion status
    and points earned for each preparation game.
    Total points and completed levels are calculated
    from these records to display the child's
    personal scoreboard. No information about
    clinical performance or visual ability is
    collected through the games.

    \item \textbf{Data Storage and Protection:}
    User preferences, selected character information,
    and game progress shall be stored locally on the
    user's device and protected from unauthorized
    access by other applications. The application
    shall not transmit this information to external
    servers or require user accounts.

    \item \textbf{Data Retention and Deletion:}
    Locally stored information is retained until
    deleted by the caregiver or through application
    removal, subject to the device's backup and
    restoration behaviour. Caregivers can clear
    stored preferences, character selections,
    and game progress through the application's
    data management functionality.

    \item \textbf{Consent and Access:}
    The application does not require registration
    or the collection of personal information
    to access its preparation games. All games
    are available to children regardless of
    whether previous levels have been completed.

    \item \textbf{Scoreboard Privacy:}
    The scoreboard displays only the child's
    own accumulated points and journey progress.
    It does not compare scores with other users
    or require an online leaderboard.

    \item \textbf{Research and Regulatory Compliance:}
    Any personal information collected separately
    for research or usability evaluation is outside
    the application's operational data storage
    and is subject to applicable institutional

\section{Rationale}

\subsection{Data Requirements}

The data requirements were developed by considering how
ophthalmology procedures and their preparation games should
be represented. Initially, we considered a one to many
relationship where each procedure could have multiple
preparation activities. However, since we had not established
whether multiple distinct games would be developed for
each procedure, we chose a simpler one to one relationship.
Each procedure is represented by one game, which may contain
multiple smaller exercises or rounds.

We also considered two approaches for selecting preparation
games: allowing caregivers to manually choose games based
on clinical recommendations, or having caregivers enter a
clinician provided code to automatically select the appropriate
games. We consulted our supervisor to determine which approach
would be more practical in a clinical setting.

Our supervisor recommended making all games accessible,
since the procedures required during an examination may
change unexpectedly. She also suggested presenting the
games as a journey with levels, rewards, a personal
scoreboard, and character customization.

Based on this feedback, we removed the caregiver configuration
and recommendation code entities from the data requirements.
We instead introduced entities to support character selection,
game progress, journey progression, and rewards. All user
data is stored locally to maintain offline functionality
and minimize the collection of personal information.
